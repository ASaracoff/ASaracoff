3.2 Persuasive Arguments

Case Study (p217)

What are we going to do about Guns? speech by President Obama on January 16, 2014.

Several ways the President and Vice President attemp to persuade people in these speeches.

  1. They attempt to persuade people about basic facts regarding gun violence or gun regulation
    • Obama believes gun violence is increasing, he has to convice peole that this is the case
    • If Vice President believes automatic weapons are the cause of so many deaths, he has to prove that this cause-and-effect relationship is true
  2. the President tried to convince people about particular topics that are right or wrong, good or bad, moral or immoral:
    • argued that the Second Amendment is important to protect
    • also attempted to persuade listeners that it is wrong to believe the Amendment endorses military-style weapons
  3. He Attempted to persuade lawmakers and lobbyists that a particular action must be pursued
    • He urged Congress to pass a law requiring universal background hecks for anyone attempting to buy a gun
    • Urged Congress to restore ban on military-style assault weapons
    • This type of persuasion is directed at particular steps that need to be taken - laws that must be found
    • A question of what should or should not be done

These speeches also highlight, once again, the importance of audience-centeredness. The President and VP were addressing citizens and lawmakers who hold a wide variety of attitudes and beliefs about guns and the Second Amendment. The two sides of the argument are that some people view any regulation as a potential violation of the Second Amendment rights, while the other side want guns to me outlawed altogether. Some people are passionate about a topic while others show very little interest in the same topic. The wide variety of attitudes, beliefs, levels of knowledge, and levels of interest create challenges for speakers who wish to persuade their audience.

This chapter will highlight some of the characteristics of persuasive speaking and some of the strategies for persuading audiences.

What makes a Persuasive Speech Different from and Informational Speech?

  • Informationl - goal is to be an expert on a topic. Attempt to provide information the audience may not have in order to help in their understanding of a topic.
  • Persuasive - goal is to advocate for something. You are promoting a specific idea or position
  • Should not be neutral - have a clear position on the topic
  • Persuasion refers to the process of creating, influencing, changing, or reinforcing people's beliefs, attitudes, values, and actions.
  • - occurs when disagreement or choice exists
  • Persuasive speaking should NOT include manipulation or coercion
  • Manipulation - refers to getting other to believe or do something through deception, misleading information, or other underhanded tactics
  • Coercion - uses implied or direct threats, insults, or physical and psychological violence in order to get the audience to comply
  • Neither of these strategies represents persuasion
  • Persuasion means that the audience is free to choose

What Would I Neeed to Persuade People About?

Three types of persuasion

What is a Question of Fact?

A speech about question of fact is a speech about somethingthat does not have a definitive or absolyte answer.

  • We cannot know for certain if the answer is right or wrong
  • Need more information that is not available at this time or needs some more speculation
  • QoF might focus on whether something DID or DID NOT happen
  • Or focus on cause and effect
  • in all QoF speeches the goal is to reinforce or change what people think, but not to change directly their actions and behaviors

What is Question of Value

Question of value speeches ask the audience to make a value judgement.

  • Persuade the audience to believe that something is good or bad, right or wrongm ethical or unethical, funny or serious, beutiful or ugly, moral or immoral etc
  • Question of Value
    • asks the audience to evaluate something's work or significance or quality
    • asks the audience to change their attitude toward something
    • has to be justified and supported
  • Speaker's position must be based on an opinion, on more than likes and dislikes
  • Speaker must provide standards for judgement
  • Speaker would first have to explain what standards make that opinion great
  • If speaker does not provide evidence and explanations for her standards and judgements, then they will not effectively persuade their audience

What is a Question of Policy?

A speech that addresses a question of policy advocated for a specific course of action or proposes a specific solution to a problem

  • Action might be signing a petition, changing a behavior or habit, passing a law, implementing a new rule at work, etc
  • The policies areusually formal rules or laws for governments, schools, businessesm or other organizations
  • QoP can also address individualor group actions that do not require formal lawsor codes
  • QoP might also try to persuade people to take more general action at the societal and cultural level
    • Ex. Nutrition expert might advocate that all consumers should read nutrition labels and ingredient lists more carefully

How Do I Decide of I Am Givine a Speech about Facts, Values, or Policies?

It can be hard to determine what the primary focus of your speeech should bem especially because the threee persuasive purposes often lead to one another. In other words: in order to persuade an audience to take a ertain action (QoP), you often have to convince then that something is right or wron, moral or immoral (QoV). And even if you are only trying to persuade an audience about the truth of certain data (QoF), it is difficult not to at least imply that they should do something about it (QoP)

  • Effective speech will often identify a primary perpersuasive purpose for the speech and allow that purpose to guide the composition and organization of the speech
  • When you compose a persuasive speech, you have to think about which purposes you want to emphasize
  • It is wise to focus on only one of the persuasive purposes as the central goal of the speech

Questions a speaker must ask is:

  • What is the primary purpose of this speech>
  • Should the speech be written to address question of fact? value? policy?
  • More than one purpose?

QoF would try to persuade the audience to accept certain facts surrounding the issue. Ex. "Carbon emissions from manufacturing lead to global climate change".

QoV would focus on a claim such as "Global climate change is a crisis that we cannot pass on to future generations."

A QoP might advocate for a particular law meant to reduce carbon emissions or attempt to persuade more consumers to drive electric vehicles.

In the end, you should let your audience guide the direction of your speech

  • agreres - focus on actions they should take or actions to avoid
  • disagrees - if the topic isabout the morality of an issue, you might not be able to persuade them to take any actions or shift their deeply help values

Best persuasive speech might be one that attempts to persuade the audience to think about the facts differently

All speaking situations, there is not a formula or prescription you can use to determint the "right" persuasive purpose. Think carefully about your audience and your reasons for speaking and let those variables guide your speech composition process

  • Audience + reasons for speaking = guide your composition process

Once I Know the Purpose of My Speech, How Can I Effectively Persuade My Audience?

Persuasion often requires getting the audience to change their attitude towards something.

  • Audience take special action? Use messages that create a favorable attitude toward your proposal
  • Audience to accept something as immoral? create and unfavorable attitude

Change in attitude isn't simple - persuasion requires evidence and justification. Speaker mus prove themself in order to convince the audience to accept the proposition. Speaker must always make an argument.

Ethos - credibility. Components:

  • Good Sense - Do your arguments make sense to your audience? Does your audience have enough information to understand your position and its significance?Is what you advocate logical? Does the effect follow from the cause, for example?
  • Good Moral Character - Do your arguments represent values that your audience finds compeling? Does your speech advocate a position that puts you in high esteem with your audience? Are you communicating ethically? Is your evidence sound? Does your speech represent your position fairly?
  • Goodwill - Do you have your audience's best interests in mind? Have you demonstrated audience-centeredness even as you advocate your position?

Pathos - emotional appeals

  • creating or crafting one emotional state (ex. fear,pain, anger) and then proposing a solution that wqould create the opposite emotional state (ex. pleasure, joy, calm).
